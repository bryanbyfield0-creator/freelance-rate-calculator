How it works

  1. Set your goal. Enter the take-home income you want and what your business costs to run each year.
  2. Be honest about time. Subtract weeks off and count only hours you can actually bill clients.
  3. Get your rates. See your minimum and recommended hourly rate, day rate and revenue targets, then price a project.

The formula

Your take-home income is what's left after tax, so we first gross it up: pre-tax profit = take-home ÷ (1 − tax rate). Add expenses to get the revenue you need: revenue = pre-tax profit + expenses.

Billable hours per year are (52 − weeks off) × billable hours per week. Then:

  • Minimum rate = revenue ÷ billable hours per year
  • Recommended rate = minimum rate × (1 + buffer %)
  • Day rate = recommended rate × 8
  • Weekly target = recommended rate × billable hours per week (for each working week)
  • Monthly target = recommended rate × billable hours per year ÷ 12
  • Project price = estimated hours × recommended rate × 1.15

This is a simplified model. Taxes are treated as a flat rate on profit, which is an approximation.

FAQ

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Add your desired take-home income (grossed up for tax) to your yearly business expenses, then divide by the number of hours you can actually bill in a year: (52 minus weeks off) times billable hours per week. Add a profit buffer on top.

How many billable hours should I plan for?

Most freelancers bill roughly 50–70% of the hours they work. The rest goes to admin, marketing, invoicing and finding clients. If you work 40 hours a week, 20–28 billable hours is a realistic starting point.

What tax rate should I use?

The calculator defaults to 25% as a rough estimate covering income and self-employment taxes. Your real rate depends on where you live and your situation, so check with a tax professional. This tool is not tax advice.

Should I charge hourly or per project?

Both can work. Knowing your hourly rate lets you price projects with confidence: estimate the hours, multiply by your rate, and add a cushion for scope creep. The project helper on this page adds 15% by default.

What is a day rate and how is it calculated here?

A day rate is a flat fee for a full working day. This calculator uses your recommended hourly rate multiplied by 8 hours.

Why add a profit buffer?

A buffer covers slow months, late payments, unpaid revisions and growth like training or new equipment. Even 10–20% makes your income far less fragile than charging the bare minimum.
